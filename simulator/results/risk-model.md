# Foreign-object risk model (per billion vehicle-km)

Generated by `node risk-model.js`. How often a vehicle on an SDR motorway hits an object hard enough to stop dead (a "brick-wall event"), and how many collisions follow. Every input that nobody has measured is given as a low, central and high value. The numbers are orders of magnitude, not predictions.

## Ratios between scenarios

The object rate and the heavy share multiply every scenario alike, so they do not change the ratios. Ranges are over every combination of low and high inputs.

| Comparison | Central | Range | Before item 2.8 |
|---|---|---|---|

| load monitoring (C ÷ D) | 1.7× | 1.4–3.9× | 3.8× |

The object rate and the heavy share move every scenario by the same factor: they set the level, not the comparison between scenarios. The high object rate is a ceiling (every item removed, most never in a lane), not a likely value.

A design question rather than an input: C and D assume the road confirms a load falling off a truck fast enough for the long gap behind it. In the simulator 0.5 s was fast enough and 1 s (what design decision D9 asks for) was not. With 1 s, central inputs:

| Case | C | D |
|---|---|---|
| Road confirms in time (as assumed above) | 4.7 | 2.8 |
| Road too slow; the follower's own sensor still catches 90 % | 5.0 | 2.9 |
| Road too slow; the follower's own sensor does not help (as in the item 1.2 tests) | 7.5 | 4.3 |

For scale: GB motorways had about 76 casualties per billion vehicle-miles in 2023 (≈ 47 per billion vehicle-km, all causes; secondary source); US road-debris crashes were ≈ 10 per billion vehicle-km on all roads (AAA Foundation, 2011–14).
