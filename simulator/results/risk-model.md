# Foreign-object risk model (per billion vehicle-km)

Generated by `node risk-model.js`. How often a vehicle hits an object hard enough to stop dead, on an SDR motorway and on today's road, and how many collisions follow. Every input that nobody has measured is given as a low, central and high value. The numbers are orders of magnitude, not predictions.

Collisions per event, from the simulator (4,200 + 600 veh/h, 10 % trucks):

- after a brick-wall stop (the vehicle that stops dead plus the followers that hit it): absolute 1.0, relative 1.9, hybrid 1.5;
- after a lost load that stops dead in front of a follower at a relative gap: 2.4 (a collision in 100 % of events); when the truck reports the loss itself at once: 2.4 (100 %);
- today's road: 5.0 per lost load (a collision in 93 % of events) and 0.17 per object lying 150 m ahead of a driver (10 %).

Source: simulator results of 2026-11-20 04:05 (section 2, 10 brick-wall runs per policy); per hazard from section 20a of 2026-11-20 16:22 (30 lost-load and 30 brick-wall runs per road).

Since item 6.1 a lost load followed at a relative gap counts the collisions measured for lost loads, not those of a brick-wall stop, and a loss the vehicle reports itself only prevents collisions behind a long gap: at a relative gap the follower is too close to stop, whoever knows. The last column shows each scenario as counted before.

For scale: the model puts today's road at 25 object collisions per billion vehicle-km (central; 5.8–217 over all inputs). Recorded: US road-debris crashes ≈ 10 per billion vehicle-km on all roads (AAA Foundation, 2011–14; a crash can hold several collisions, and most debris does not stop a car dead); GB motorways had about 76 casualties per billion vehicle-miles in 2023 (≈ 47 per billion vehicle-km, all causes; secondary source).
